Two of the county's biggest Saturday farmers markets close for the season on November 21. That leaves eight Saturdays to shop the Doylestown and Wrightstown markets at their fall peak, when apples, winter squash, pumpkins and the last tomatoes share the same tables.

The farm stands keep longer hours than the markets, and several stay open all year. We checked 17 Bucks County farms and markets against their own websites for addresses, hours and what they sell. The full list sits on our Food Safety Watch page. What follows is how to use it this fall.

Fewer hands between the field and your counter

Federal recall notices this season have covered ground cinnamon, applesauce, frozen pasta and mangoes, all of which moved through importers, distributors and store shelves across several states before anyone caught a problem. A farm stand doesn't make food risk-free, and no grower on this list claims that. What a farm stand does offer is a short supply chain and a person behind the counter who can answer questions about how the crop or the animal was raised.

Ask those questions. Growers who sell face to face expect them, and the answers vary a lot from farm to farm. Some farms carry third-party certification. Others describe their practices in their own words. The distinction is worth knowing before you pay a premium.

Upper Bucks: year-round stores and pastured meat

Trauger's Farm Market sits at Route 611 and Island Road in Kintnersville. The family describes it as a 60-acre fruit and vegetable farm with an on-farm store that's open year-round. Fall hours run Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. and weekends 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the farm says pumpkins and fall vegetables are coming in now.

For meat, Bolton's Farm Market at 1005 E. Main Street in Silverdale sells turkey, chicken and beef that the family raises on the property and describes as antibiotic- and hormone-free. The market is open Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Saturday until 4 p.m. If you want a fresh turkey for late November, this is the month to ask about ordering.

Hershberger Heritage Farm, at 1614 Ridge Road in Sellersville, sells grass-fed beef, pasture-raised heritage pork, chicken and eggs. The farm says its cattle are rotationally grazed. The store is closed Tuesdays and Thursdays.

Blooming Glen Farm in Perkasie states that it is 100% certified organic and grows vegetables, fruit, flowers and herbs on 40 acres. It sells mainly through its CSA and at seasonal farmers markets. The borough-run Perkasie Farmers Market runs Saturdays 9 a.m. to noon through October 31, and the indoor Quakertown Farmers Market at 201 Station Road is open Friday through Sunday.

Central Bucks: orchards, cider and two producer markets

Solebury Orchards, at 3325 Creamery Road outside New Hope, grows apples, peaches and blueberries and presses its own cider. It's open Thursday to Sunday 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. through October, with pick-your-own in season, then closes an hour earlier in November and December.

Also in Solebury Township, Manoff Market Gardens and Cidery at 3157 Comfort Road is open year-round, Wednesday to Saturday 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. and Monday and Tuesday until 3 p.m. Roots to River Farm at 3175 North Sugan Road calls itself a small, diversified, transitional organic farm. It opens Wednesday 3 to 7 p.m. and Saturday noon to 5 p.m. and sells CSA shares.

In Buckingham, None Such Farm Market at 4458 York Road carries produce, beef, prepared foods and baked goods Tuesday to Saturday, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. The farm describes itself as a 100% non-GMO grower.

The Doylestown Farmers Market sets up on South Hamilton Street on Saturdays from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. The Wrightstown Farmers Market runs Saturdays 9 a.m. to noon at the Middletown Grange Fairgrounds, 576 Penns Park Road. The Bucks County Foodshed Alliance, which is tied to both markets, describes Wrightstown as producer-only, with 35 to 40 farmers and makers each week, and says it accepts SNAP EBT benefits. In Newtown, Milk House Farm Market at 1118 Slack Road is open every day from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. with pasture-raised eggs, produce, chicken, turkey and pork.

Lower Bucks: pick-your-own within reach of I-295

Styer Orchard at 97 Styers Lane in Langhorne lists Cortland, Jonagold and Mutsu apples for picking at $2.49 a pound, with limited availability, and a pumpkin patch that's open every day, weather permitting. Bring your own bags. The farm doesn't allow sampling in the orchard.

Two farms share Stony Hill Road in Yardley. Shady Brook Farm at number 931 runs a market that stays open until 10 p.m. on Fridays and Saturdays and stocks produce grown on the farm alongside local honey, eggs and milk in glass bottles. Charlann Farms at number 586 grows sweet corn, tomatoes, peppers, melons and pumpkins, and its fall festival started September 19.

In Morrisville, Snipes Farm and Education Center is a nonprofit at 890 West Bridge Street that states it is certified organic. Its market schedule is limited, so confirm the hours before you go.

What the labels on the chalkboard mean

"Certified organic" is a regulated term. A farm that uses it has been inspected under the USDA's National Organic Program. Blooming Glen and Snipes both state on their own sites that they hold that certification.

"Transitional organic," the phrase Roots to River uses, generally describes land being managed to organic standards during the waiting period before certification. Terms like "pasture-raised," "non-GMO" and "antibiotic- and hormone-free" are the farm's own descriptions of how it works. They can be perfectly accurate, but they aren't the same as a third-party seal, so it's fair to ask what each one means on that particular farm.

One more habit pays off at any stand. Wash produce under running water before you eat it, and keep meat, eggs and dairy cold on the drive home. A cooler in the trunk is enough.

Quick answers

Which Bucks County farm markets stay open after Thanksgiving?

Trauger's in Kintnersville and Manoff Market Gardens in New Hope both say they're open year-round, and Milk House Farm Market in Newtown says it's open every day of the year. Solebury Orchards runs Thursday to Sunday through December.

When do the outdoor farmers markets end?

Doylestown and Wrightstown both run through Saturday, November 21. Perkasie's market runs through October 31.

Can I use SNAP benefits at a farmers market?

The Wrightstown Farmers Market accepts SNAP EBT, according to the Bucks County Foodshed Alliance. Ask at the information table at other markets, since policies differ.

Is food from a farm stand covered by recalls?

Yes. Farms are subject to the same federal and state food laws, and recalls can involve produce and meat from operations of any size. Keep checking the FDA and USDA recall pages, which we track on Food Safety Watch.
