Since December 1, 2025, nobody has given birth at St. Luke's Grand View Hospital in Sellersville. St. Luke's paused labor and delivery there over staffing and sent every birth to its Upper Bucks Campus in Quakertown. Changes like that rarely make the front page, but they decide where thousands of women in this county drive when it matters most.

Bucks County has no shortage of care. What it lacks is one place that lays it all out. This guide covers OB/GYN practices, breast imaging, birth units, pelvic floor therapy, menopause care, postpartum mental health and free or low-cost options, region by region and need by need. Every address and phone number below came from the provider's own website or an official source. Hours, insurance networks and services change often, so call ahead to confirm before you go.

Your Annual Visit, From Upper Bucks to the River

Most women's health starts with a gynecologist you see once a year and call when something feels off. The county's large health systems each run or partner with OB/GYN practices, and several independent offices fill in between.

Central Bucks

Doylestown Health lists three affiliated gynecology practices on its site. Doylestown Women's Health Center is at 708 N. Shady Retreat Road, Suite 7, in Doylestown (215-340-2229). It's the largest of the three, with five physicians, four advanced practice clinicians and a certified nurse midwife on staff.

Doylestown Gynecology sits at 1456 Ferry Road in Doylestown (215-348-2992). Bucks County Women's Wellness is at 2325 Heritage Center Drive, Suite 317, in Furlong (215-874-4200), and its services run from well-woman exams and PCOS treatment to fibroid care and menopause management.

Upper Bucks

St. Luke's University Health Network dominates the north end of the county. St. Luke's OB/GYN Stoneridge is at 1532 Park Avenue, Suite 101, in Quakertown (267-985-5100). The practice combines physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants and certified nurse midwives, and lists menopause management among its specialties.

St. Luke's OB/GYN Grand View sees patients at 99 N. West End Boulevard, Suite 104, in Quakertown (215-536-3200). It highlights adolescent care, pregnancy and menopause, and the same phone number also serves its Dublin office at 145 N. Main Street, Suite 400.

Lower Bucks

Trinity Health Mid-Atlantic runs Ob/Gyn Langhorne in the St. Clare Building at 1203 Langhorne-Newtown Road, Suite 336 (215-322-5042), next to St. Mary Medical Center. The same number books its Feasterville office at 178 West Street Road. Jefferson Health also lists a Jefferson OB/GYN practice in Langhorne; check its page or call Jefferson for the current address before your first visit.

Mammograms Close to Home

Every major health system serving the county offers 3D mammography. Many centers require an order from your doctor, so ask for one at your annual visit.

  • Doylestown Health Clark Center for Breast Imaging operates at Doylestown Hospital, 595 W. State Street, Doylestown, and at 847 Easton Road in Warrington. Both locations book through 215-345-2274.
  • Jefferson-Solis Mammography, Doylestown is at 4369 W. Swamp Road in the Cross Keys Place shopping center (215-453-7661). It offers 3D screening and automated breast ultrasound for women with dense tissue.
  • Jefferson-Solis Mammography, Bucks is inside Jefferson Bucks Hospital, 380 N. Oxford Valley Road, Langhorne (215-757-3013). It handles screening and diagnostic mammograms, breast ultrasound and bone density scans, with evening hours until 7 p.m. Monday through Thursday.
  • St. Mary Breast Center in Langhorne schedules at 215-710-2208. It covers the full arc from 3D screening through ultrasound, MRI, biopsy and surgical consults, with a nurse navigator assigned after an abnormal result.
  • Lower Bucks Hospital, 501 Bath Road in Bristol, offers 3D mammography, ultrasound and bone density testing. Scheduling runs weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. at 215-785-9343.
  • St. Luke's Women's Imaging at 121 S. 11th Street in Quakertown offers 3D mammography in what St. Luke's calls a Sensory Suite, plus DEXA bone density scans. Book through St. Luke's central scheduling at 1-866-785-8537.

Imaging at St. Luke's Grand View campus, 700 Lawn Avenue in Sellersville, books through the same St. Luke's central line.

Having a Baby Here

The county's three busiest birth units each keep a Level II neonatal unit on site for babies who need extra help.

The VIA Maternity Center at Doylestown Health has nine labor, delivery and recovery rooms and 22 private postpartum rooms. Water therapy is available during labor, anesthesia is staffed around the clock, and neonatologists from Children's Hospital of Philadelphia cover its intensive care nursery 24/7. Board-certified lactation consultants are on the unit every day.

The St. Luke's Upper Bucks Women & Babies Pavilion at 3000 St. Luke's Drive in Quakertown is a 118,000-square-foot building near Routes 309 and 313 and I-476. It now takes the births that used to happen at Grand View. Labor and delivery can be reached at 267-985-2099.

St. Mary Medical Center at 1201 Langhorne-Newtown Road in Langhorne runs eight private birthing suites, two surgical suites for cesareans, and a Level II NICU staffed 24/7 by CHOP neonatologists. Its obstetrics department is at 215-710-5802, and the hospital also offers a perinatal testing center for high-risk pregnancies.

Choosing a Midwife

Midwifery care in Bucks County mostly happens inside hospital-affiliated practices. St. Luke's OB/GYN Stoneridge employs certified nurse midwives, and Doylestown Women's Health Center has a CNM on its team. Families who want a freestanding birth center will likely be driving. The Birth Center at Lifecycle WomanCare in Bryn Mawr (610-525-6086) calls itself the first licensed birth center in Pennsylvania and is accredited by the Commission for the Accreditation of Birth Centers.

After the Baby Arrives

The Bucks County Department of Health runs a free Maternal Child Health home visiting program for every pregnant woman, new parent and infant guardian living in the county. A nurse comes to your home and covers feeding, infant care, safety, nutrition and depression. Call 215-345-3344 in Doylestown and Quakertown or 267-580-3510 in Levittown.

Postpartum depression and anxiety are common and treatable. Postpartum Support International's Pennsylvania chapter connects parents with trained clinicians, peer support and online groups. Its HelpLine takes calls and texts in English and Spanish at 1-800-944-4773. The National Maternal Mental Health Hotline, 1-833-852-6262 (1-833-TLC-MAMA), answers by call or text around the clock and is free and confidential.

Bladder, Pelvic Pain and Recovery

Leaking when you laugh, pain during sex and a stubborn gap in the abdominal wall after pregnancy all have the same first-line fix, which is a physical therapist trained in the pelvic floor.

Doylestown Health's pelvic floor program treats incontinence, prolapse, endometriosis pain, vulvodynia, diastasis recti and recovery after hysterectomy. You'll need a physician referral, and sessions typically run 50 to 60 minutes once a week. For surgical options, Doylestown Health lists a urogynecology line at 215-230-0600.

St. Luke's Physical Therapy offers pelvic health care in Bucks at its Sellersville and Ottsville locations, and Ottsville adds dedicated postpartum rehab. Vivid Women's Health, an independent practice, treats pregnancy, postpartum and perimenopause pelvic issues and fits pessaries at 790 Newtown-Yardley Road, Suite 425, in Newtown and 252 W. Swamp Road, Suite 23, in Doylestown (267-685-6368).

The Midlife Shift

Hot flashes and lost sleep send a lot of women searching for a specialist, and many don't realize their gynecologist may already treat it. Doylestown Women's Health Center offers hormone therapy, vaginal estrogen and lifestyle counseling for menopause. Bucks County Women's Wellness in Furlong and both St. Luke's practices in Quakertown list menopause management among their core services. Ask directly when you book, since some providers focus on it far more than others.

When Money Is the Obstacle

Pennsylvania's Breast and Cervical Cancer Early Detection Program pays for mammograms, breast MRI, Pap and HPV tests and follow-up care for uninsured and underinsured residents. Breast screening covers ages 40 to 64 and cervical screening covers ages 21 to 64. Through 2027, a single adult earning up to $39,900 a year qualifies, as does a family of four earning up to $82,500. The state says women and transgender people who have or had breasts or a cervix may be eligible. In Bucks County, enroll through AccessMatters at 1-800-848-3367.

The Ann Silverman Community Health Clinic at 595 W. State Street in Doylestown provides free medical and dental care to uninsured, low-income residents. Call 215-345-2260 to be screened for eligibility.

Planned Parenthood's Bensalem Health Center is where Planned Parenthood Keystone directed Bucks patients after its Warminster center closed in June 2024. It's at 2185 Galloway Road in the Centre Plaza Shopping Center (215-638-0629), and it sets fees based on income. Services include birth control, STI testing, wellness screenings, prenatal and postpartum care and gender-affirming care, with Spanish-language service and telehealth available.

The Bucks County Department of Health runs an HIV and STD screening program and immunization services. Its main office is at 1282 Almshouse Road in Doylestown (215-345-3318). In Bensalem, St. Mary Family Medicine (215-245-8873) offers full-service family medicine at 3331 Street Road.

Federally qualified health centers charge on a sliding scale by law. Search by ZIP code on the federal health center finder to see the closest ones to your home.

If You Don't Feel Safe

Health care includes safety. A Woman's Place has served Bucks County since 1976 with emergency shelter, counseling, legal advocacy and rehousing for anyone experiencing intimate partner violence. Its free, confidential hotline answers 24 hours a day at 1-800-220-8116, and friends and family can call too.

The Network of Victim Assistance supports victims of sexual assault and other crimes with forensic exams, counseling and court advocacy at no cost. Its 24/7 helpline is 1-800-675-6900. If you're in immediate danger, call 911.

Questions Readers Ask

Do I need a referral for a mammogram?

Usually, yes. Most centers, including the Jefferson-Solis locations, require an order from a doctor for a screening mammogram. If you don't have a doctor, the state's early detection program can connect you with one.

Can I still deliver at Grand View in Sellersville?

Not right now. St. Luke's paused labor and delivery there on December 1, 2025, and moved births to its Upper Bucks Campus in Quakertown.

I'm uninsured. Where do I start?

Call AccessMatters at 1-800-848-3367 for free breast and cervical screening, and call the Ann Silverman clinic at 215-345-2260 for primary care. Planned Parenthood in Bensalem also charges based on income.

Where can trans and nonbinary patients find affirming care?

Planned Parenthood's Bensalem center lists gender-affirming care among its services, and the state's screening program explicitly covers transgender people who have or had breasts or a cervix. For other practices, ask when you book.
