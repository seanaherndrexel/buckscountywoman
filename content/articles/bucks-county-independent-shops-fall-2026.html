Three makers markets land on the same day this month. On Saturday, October 17, the Craftery Market sets up at the Mercer Museum in Doylestown, Autumn Alive closes Broad Street in Quakertown, and Historic Bristol Day fills the riverfront borough, all starting at 10 a.m.

That Saturday is the busiest shopping date on the county's fall calendar, but it isn't the only reason to leave the big-box lots behind. We checked 14 independent shops and markets against their own pages for addresses, hours and what's on the shelves this month. No shop on this list describes itself as woman-owned on its website, so we haven't labeled any that way. Where a store names its owners, we've passed that along.

Five counters where the staff has read the book

The Doylestown Bookshop at 16 S. Main Street has sold new books since 1998, along with cards, toys and gifts. It's open Monday to Wednesday 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., Thursday to Saturday until 8 p.m., and Sunday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. The shop says Lauren and Damian Ford, longtime Bucks County residents, bought it in September 2023. Its October calendar is full: a Local Author Expo runs Saturday, October 24, from 1 to 3 p.m., and novelist Anna North reads from "Bog Queen" on Monday, October 19, at 6 p.m.

Its sister store, The Lahaska Bookshop, sits at 162 A Route 263 inside Peddler's Village and keeps village hours: Monday to Thursday 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., Friday and Saturday until 8 p.m., Sunday 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Newtown Bookshop, at 2120 South Eagle Road, keeps a full club calendar. This month's list includes a nonfiction club on Wednesday, October 14, at 1 p.m., a daytime club on Thursday, October 15, at 11 a.m., and a cookbook club on Wednesday, October 21, at 6:30 p.m. Hours are Monday and Tuesday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., Wednesday and Thursday until 7 p.m., Friday until 4 p.m., Saturday until 5 p.m., and Sunday 11 a.m. to 3 p.m.

In New Hope, Farley's Bookshop has been at 44 South Main Street since 1971. Jim and Nancy Farley bought the business in 1967, and the shop's own history says the family later sold it to three longtime employees, Buffy Hastings, Katie Hastings and Charlie Balfour. We couldn't confirm current hours from the store's site, so call or check before you drive.

Yardley's Commonplace Reader is listed by the regional booksellers association at 49 S. Main Street. The Bucks County Herald has reported that owner Liz Young stocks two rooms of children's books and hosts six monthly book clubs, including one for women's literary fiction. Hours weren't available to us, so confirm those too.

Wool, mums and a Betsey Johnson dress

Forever Yarn, at 68 South Main Street in Doylestown, has sold natural-fiber yarn, kits and pattern books since 2003. Walk-in hours are short and worth memorizing: Thursday 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., Friday 11 a.m. to 3 p.m., Saturday 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Sunday noon to 4 p.m. Tuesday and Wednesday are by appointment, and the shop is closed Monday. Sunday afternoons double as an open knitting circle, and the store offers classes at every level.

North of the borough, Bucks Country Gardens at 1057 N. Easton Road is open every day from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The greenhouse and nursery carry fall mums and pansies, and the building also holds a boutique and a furniture department. A succulent pumpkin workshop is scheduled for Tuesday, November 3, from 6:30 to 8 p.m. The fee is $59.99, and space is limited.

For clothing with a past, Ditto Vintage opened its storefront at 20 W. Bridge Street in New Hope in May 2023. Owner Jessica Schuyler, who started the business in 2017, sells vintage and secondhand pieces mostly from the 1960s through the early 2000s. Prices on the shop's site this week ranged from $45 for a tank top to $150 for a designer tee. The site still shows its summer schedule of noon to 6 p.m. most days, noon to 7 p.m. Saturday, and closed Wednesday.

In Perkasie, the borough's gift card directory lists Bloom Flower Co. at 5 N. 7th Street for fresh arrangements and custom designs. Call 215-257-4080 for hours.

Brick paths and brass bands in Lahaska

Peddler's Village, at Route 202 and Street Road, keeps its shops open Monday to Thursday 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., Friday and Saturday until 8 p.m., and Sunday 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. through November 19. More than 100 handmade scarecrows line the walkways into the last week of October, and looking is free.

This weekend is the village's 10th OctoberFeast, Saturday and Sunday, October 10 and 11, from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Admission and parking are free. Expect brass bands, food trucks and crowds, so shop early if you want elbow room. Stores stay open until 8 p.m. Saturday.

Four Saturdays' worth of makers in two weekends

Historic Fallsington Day comes first, on Saturday, October 10. The free, day-long program puts crafters, vendors and food along Yardley Avenue in Fallsington. Park at Fallsington Elementary School and walk in.

A week later, the Craftery Market at the Mercer Museum brings more than 50 vendors to 84 S. Pine Street in Doylestown on Saturday, October 17, from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. Admission to the market is free.

The same day, Autumn Alive runs from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. in downtown Quakertown. Broad Street closes to traffic from 4th Street to Hellertown Avenue for local crafters, small businesses, an authors' corner, a food court and a costumed pet parade. The rain date is Saturday, October 24. While you're there, the downtown group's directory lists Sine's 5 & 10 at 236 W. Broad Street and Moyer's shoe store at 316 W. Broad.

Historic Bristol Day also falls on October 17, from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. across Bristol Borough. The organizers list artisan vendors among the day's attractions and sell $20 tickets. Their page doesn't spell out what the ticket covers, so read it before you go.

Quick answers

Which shops on this list are open on Sundays?

Both Doylestown and Lahaska bookshops, Newtown Bookshop, Forever Yarn, Bucks Country Gardens and the Peddler's Village shops all post Sunday hours. Ditto Vintage listed Sunday hours of noon to 6 p.m. on its summer schedule.

Are the October craft markets free?

The Craftery Market, Historic Fallsington Day and OctoberFeast all say admission is free. Autumn Alive is an open street festival and its page doesn't list an admission charge. Historic Bristol Day sells $20 tickets.

What happens if it rains on October 17?

Autumn Alive moves to Saturday, October 24. The Mercer Museum's listing for the Craftery Market doesn't name a rain date, so check the museum's event page that morning.

Is there another makers market before the holidays?

Yes. The Mercer Museum lists a Holiday Market for Saturday, December 12, from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with more than 40 vendors selling handmade goods and original art. Admission is free.
