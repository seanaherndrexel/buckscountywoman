About 3,600 people a year end up in a U.S. emergency room with a Halloween-related injury, and 48% of those injuries come from carving pumpkins. That figure is from the Consumer Product Safety Commission, which also found that more than half of the injured, 56%, were adults. The knife on the kitchen table is a bigger hazard than anything in the candy bag.

This year the calendar does households a favor and plays one trick. Halloween falls on Saturday, October 31, so there's no school the next morning. Daylight saving time ends a few hours later, at 2 a.m. on Sunday, November 1. Here's a planner for the three weeks in between.

What the towns have posted, and what they haven't

We went looking for 2026 trick-or-treat hours and parade dates on borough, township and police pages across the county. As of October 8, very few have published them.

Perkasie Borough has posted its proclamation naming Saturday, October 31, 2026 as Trick-or-Treat Night. Check the borough's news page for the hours. Confirm this year's notice before you plan around it.

Quakertown Borough's Halloween parade page still showed the October 2025 date when we checked, so we're not listing a 2026 date. We also couldn't find posted 2026 hours on official pages for Doylestown, Newtown, Bristol, Bensalem, Warminster or the other municipalities we searched. Several of those events appear on community calendars, but we only list what a town has published itself. Call your municipal office or check its police department page in the next two weeks. Most Bucks County towns that set hours announce them by mid-October.

One verified daytime option: Pennsbury Manor, the state historic site at 400 Pennsbury Memorial Road in Morrisville, holds Tricks and Treats on Sunday, October 25, from 1 to 4 p.m. Admission is free, registration is required through the site's online form, and children must be with an adult. Daylight trick-or-treating suits toddlers and kids who find dark streets and crowds overwhelming.

The school week around the 31st

District calendars change the shape of Halloween week more than the holiday does. These dates come from each district's own 2026 to 2027 calendar.

  • Central Bucks: early dismissal on Friday, October 30, with a teacher in-service in the afternoon.
  • Neshaminy: half day on Friday, October 30. No school Tuesday, November 3, and the district is closed Wednesday, November 11. The elementary conference day is December 11.
  • Pennsbury: no students on Monday, November 2 (grading day) or Tuesday, November 3 (teacher workshop). No school Wednesday, November 11. Conferences bring half days for kindergarten through grade 8 on November 23 and 24.
  • Pennridge: no school for students on Monday, October 12.

We couldn't confirm late-October dates on the Council Rock, Bensalem, Quakertown or Palisades district sites, so check those calendars directly. All districts note that dates can change.

For Central Bucks and Neshaminy families, the Friday half day means an afternoon to fill. It's a good slot for the costume dress rehearsal.

Costumes and candy without the October markup

Set the number first. Decide what the household will spend on costumes, candy and decorations combined, then divide it. A budget made in the store aisle is always larger.

Start costumes from the closet. A costume built on clothes a child already owns, such as sweatpants, a hoodie and rain boots, needs only one or two purchased pieces. Thrift shops and neighborhood swap groups fill the gaps, and a swap among three or four families costs nothing.

Whatever you assemble, the federal guidance on fit and fabric is specific. The FDA says to choose costumes labeled flame resistant, and to use polyester or nylon for homemade ones. The CPSC adds that any fabric can burn, and that long or baggy costumes cause trips and falls. Hem anything that drags.

For candy, count before you buy. If you tracked last year's visitors, use that number. If not, ask a neighbor. Buy one bag less than you think you need, keep the receipt for unopened extras, and hold a non-candy backup in reserve.

A second bowl by the door

The Teal Pumpkin Project, run by the nonprofit Food Allergy Research and Education (FARE), asks households to offer non-food treats in a separate bowl and to signal it with a teal pumpkin or one of FARE's free printable signs. FARE says its map logged more than 12,500 locations in 2024, and you can add your address so families can plan a route.

Stickers, pencils, bubbles and glow sticks all work, and they keep until next year. Keeping the bowls separate is the point: it stops allergens from candy wrappers reaching the toys.

A few habits make a porch easier for kids with sensory or communication differences. Skip strobe lights and jump-scare sound effects near the door. Don't require a "trick or treat" or a removed mask before handing something over. Welcome older kids and adults with disabilities without comment.

Paint, lenses and the 6 o'clock hour

The FDA advises testing face paint on a small patch of the wearer's arm 24 to 48 hours ahead and watching for rash, redness or swelling. Don't let anyone sleep in it. The agency's novelty makeup page notes that no fluorescent color is approved for use near the eyes.

Decorative contact lenses are medical devices. The FDA says lenses sold without a prescription at costume shops, salons or online are not approved, and that wearing them can cause infection, injury and permanent vision loss. Teenagers are the usual buyers, so raise it now.

On the road, Safe Kids Worldwide puts the popular trick-or-treating window at 5:30 to 9:30 p.m. and says adults should accompany children under 12. AAA, citing a 2019 study in JAMA Pediatrics, reports that 6 to 7 p.m. is the deadliest hour and that children ages 4 to 8 are about 10 times more likely to be killed as pedestrians on Halloween than on other fall evenings. NHTSA says nearly half of crash deaths on Halloween night in 2023 were alcohol-related. With the holiday on a Saturday, plan the adult ride home before the party starts.

Reflective tape, a flashlight or glow stick per child, and makeup in place of a vision-blocking mask cover most of the rest.

Cider on the driveway

A neighborhood gathering doesn't need a theme. Pick one driveway, set out a folding table, and ask each household to bring one thing: a slow cooker of chili, a thermos of cider, a tray of cut fruit. Start at 5 p.m. so kids eat before they walk. The FDA suggests a snack beforehand so children are less tempted to eat unchecked candy on the route.

Label dishes with their main allergens, use battery lights in the pumpkins, and offer drinks without alcohol. If you serve cider from a farm stand, ask whether it's pasteurized.

Sunday morning, one hour richer

Clocks fall back at 2 a.m. on November 1. The CPSC ties one chore to that date: test every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm and change the batteries, unless the unit has a sealed 10-year battery. Replace any smoke alarm more than 10 years old. The agency says most carbon monoxide deaths occur between November and February, which is when furnaces come on.

Quick answers

What time is trick-or-treating in my Bucks County town?

Each municipality sets its own hours, and most hadn't posted 2026 hours by October 8. Perkasie has proclaimed October 31 as its night. For everywhere else, check your borough or township website or its police department page.

Is there school the Monday after Halloween?

It depends on the district. Pennsbury students are off Monday, November 2 and Tuesday, November 3. Neshaminy is off November 3. Central Bucks lists an early dismissal on October 30. Confirm on your district's calendar.

What do I put in a teal pumpkin bowl?

Small non-food items such as stickers, pencils, bubbles or glow sticks, kept in a bowl apart from the candy. FARE offers free signs and a map on its website.

When should I change my smoke alarm batteries?

The CPSC recommends at least once a year, and the end of daylight saving time on Sunday, November 1 is the reminder. Test alarms monthly.
