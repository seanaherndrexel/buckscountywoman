Six free guided walks and hikes, three library stitching circles and 13 county senior centers are open to Bucks County women over the next two weeks. Several need no sign-up at all, so you can decide at breakfast and be there by lunch. We checked each one against the organizer's own listing for the two weeks from Thursday, October 8 to Thursday, October 22, and we left out anything we couldn't confirm for 2026.

None of these is a business networking event. They're standing groups and guided outings where a woman who arrives alone will find people already doing something with their hands or their feet, which makes conversation a lot easier.

Bring your yarn, skip the sign-up

The Bucks County Free Library runs three fiber groups that meet inside this window.

Stitch & Knit meets at the Warminster branch every Tuesday from 1 to 3 p.m. in the Large Meeting Room, including October 13 and 20. Bring a project in progress or start a new one. The branch reference desk answers questions at 215-672-4362.

At the New Hope-Solebury branch, Stitch in Time is an adult fiber arts drop-in for knitters, crocheters and cross stitchers on Mondays from 11 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. The library lists October 12 and 19, and says no registration is required.

The same branch hosts the River Stitch Guild, a hand-sewing and textile art group, on Mondays from 2 to 4 p.m., also on October 12 and 19. Beginners are welcome and some starter supplies are on hand, but the library caps the group at eight adults, so check with the branch before you drive over.

In Bristol, the Margaret R. Grundy Memorial Library hosts a bring-your-own-book club with no assigned reading. Members pick one to three titles in the month's genre and talk about them. October's genre is banned books. The August and September sessions fell on the second Wednesday from 6:30 to 7:30 p.m. We couldn't load the October listing, so confirm the date on the library calendar before you go.

Central Bucks on foot

Peace Valley Nature Center, at 170 North Chapman Road in Doylestown, has three free outings on the calendar. Donations are accepted at all of them.

Saturday Morning Bird Walk

A local birding expert leads this two-hour walk from 8 to 10 a.m. on October 10 and October 17. It's aimed at adults who want to practice identification. No registration.

Wednesday Walkabout

This one-hour themed hike for adults steps off at 1 p.m. on October 14 and October 21. No registration here either, which makes it an easy first visit.

Birdability Outing

On Saturday, October 10 at 9:30 a.m., a certified Birdability leader guides a 90-minute outing built for birders of all mobility levels. The group meets at the wheelchair-accessible spaces in the New Galena Village parking lot, 264 Creek Road, Doylestown, and follows a paved, mostly flat trail. The center says the outing uses tactile bird models and birding by ear to support blind and low-vision participants. Registration is required through the center's website.

Runners have a Doylestown option too. The Bucks County Roadrunners list a Saturday 8 a.m. group run that leaves from the Starbucks at 10 North Main Street. The club describes it as open to all paces and typically eight miles. The club's page says visitors are always welcome. It doesn't list a fee for group runs, so ask the run leader about membership if you plan to keep coming.

Upper Bucks: one afternoon in the woods

Heritage Conservancy's Fall Foliage Hike is Saturday, October 17 from 1 to 2:30 p.m. at Fuller-Pursell Preserve, 2858 Slifer Valley Road, Riegelsville. The conservancy calls it a relaxed, beginner-friendly guided hike. It's free, but you do need to register on the event page. The rain date is Sunday, October 18.

Lower Bucks: birds, hills and a Tuesday loop

Churchville Nature Center, 501 Churchville Lane, runs its own free guided Saturday Morning Bird Walk at 8 a.m. on October 10 and 17. Expect about two hours over a mile-long round trip. On Sunday, October 18 from 1 to 2 p.m., the center offers an Autumn Changes Nature Walk as part of its free fall walk series, with sign-up through a form on the center's site.

The Roadrunners' year-round "McTuesday" run meets Tuesdays at 6 p.m. in the parking lot across from McCaffrey's, 680 Heacock Road, Morrisville. It's a no-drop run of 3.5 to 7 miles, meaning nobody gets left behind. A Saturday 7 a.m. canal path run starts at Cramer's Bakery, 16 East Afton Avenue, Yardley.

Rooms with a standing welcome

For LGBTQ+ teens and young women

Planned Parenthood Keystone's Rainbow Room is a free weekly gathering for LGBTQ+ young people ages 14 to 21. The Doylestown group meets Wednesdays from 6 to 8 p.m. at 186 East Court Street. The Lower Bucks group meets Tuesdays from 6 to 8 p.m. at 334 South Bellevue Avenue, Langhorne. On the fourth Wednesday of the month, the Doylestown group also welcomes ages 12 and 13. The program line is 267-282-4117.

For women with disabilities

The Bucks County Center for Independent Living in Fairless Hills runs peer support where people with disabilities talk with other people with disabilities. The county's listing says the in-person group meets every other Wednesday at the center's office and a virtual group meets every Friday. Call 215-781-5070 to find out which Wednesdays fall in October and how to join online.

For older women

The county supports 13 senior community centers that offer recreation and classes, and most serve a hot meal. In Upper Bucks, try the Upper Bucks Activity Center at 2183 Milford Square Pike, Quakertown (215-536-3066) or Pennridge Community Center at 146 East Main Street, Perkasie (215-453-7027). The Central Bucks Activity Center is at 700 North Shady Retreat Road, Doylestown (215-348-0565). In Lower Bucks, the Bristol Borough Area Active Adult Center is at 301 Wood Street (215-788-9238) and the Middletown Senior Citizens Center is at 2142 Trenton Road, Levittown (215-945-2920). Each center sets its own schedule, so phone for the October calendar.

Before you walk in

Library and nature center programs can change with a day's notice. Peace Valley notes that its programs are subject to cancellation for weather. Look at the listing the morning you plan to go. For the walks, wear closed shoes and carry water. For the stitching groups, a half-finished scarf is all the introduction you need.

Quick answers

Which of these can I attend without registering?

Stitch & Knit in Warminster, Stitch in Time in New Hope, both Peace Valley walks, the Churchville Saturday bird walk and the Roadrunners group runs are all listed without a sign-up step. The Birdability Outing, the Heritage Conservancy hike and Churchville's October 18 nature walk ask you to register first.

Is there anything here for someone who uses a wheelchair or has low vision?

Yes. Peace Valley's Birdability Outing on October 10 meets at accessible parking with accessible restrooms nearby and stays on a paved, mostly flat trail. The center says it is designed with blind and low-vision birders in mind. The Center for Independent Living's peer group also has a virtual meeting every Friday.

Do I have to be a member to join a Roadrunners group run?

The club's group run page says visitors are always welcome and marks several runs as open to all paces. It labels the runs as member group runs, so regulars should expect to join the club. Contact the run leader listed for each run with questions.

Are the library groups only for people who live in that town?

The listings we read set no residency rule and mention no fee. The only limit we found is the eight-adult cap on the River Stitch Guild in New Hope.
