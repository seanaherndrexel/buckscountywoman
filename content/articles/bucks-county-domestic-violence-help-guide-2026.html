One phone number in Bucks County is answered 24 hours a day by a domestic violence advocate: 1-800-220-8116. It belongs to A Woman's Place, the county's domestic violence organization, and the call is free and confidential.

October is Domestic Violence Awareness Month. We used it as a reason to check the county's safety net against Bucks County government pages, the District Attorney's resource list, PA 211 and Planned Parenthood Keystone. Every number below appears on one of those pages. If you are in immediate danger, call 911.

Before you click or call

A shared phone, tablet or computer keeps a record. If someone else can see your device, think about using a library computer, a work phone or a trusted friend's phone to look things up and make calls. A private or incognito browser window doesn't save history on that device, though it won't hide activity from monitoring software or a shared phone bill. Clearing recent calls and texts afterward is another small step. None of this is required to ask for help. An advocate can talk through what's safe for your situation.

The two local hotlines

A Woman's Place

A Woman's Place answers its hotline, 1-800-220-8116, around the clock. PA 211's listing describes emergency help after an incident, in person or virtual, along with crisis counseling, safety planning and referrals, all free of charge for people experiencing domestic violence. The WomensLaw directory lists emergency shelter, individual and group counseling, a children's program, support groups, rehousing help and court accompaniment.

The administrative office line is 215-343-9241, and the legal advocacy office is 215-348-0445. The organization's public mailing address is a post office box in Doylestown. The shelter location is confidential, and it stays that way here.

You don't have to be ready to leave to call. Advocates take calls from people who only want to talk through what's happening.

NOVA

The Network of Victim Assistance, known as NOVA, runs a second 24-hour hotline at 1-800-675-6900. The District Attorney's office describes NOVA's services as free and confidential, including counseling, court accompaniment and help filing for Pennsylvania Crime Victims' Compensation. The county lists NOVA under sexual violence and victim assistance, so it's the call to make after a sexual assault or another crime. NOVA's office line is 215-343-6543.

A court order, step by step

A protection from abuse order, usually called a PFA, is a civil court order. Bucks County's PFA page lays out the process, and you don't need a lawyer to file.

You can fill out the petition online through the county's portal or print it in English or Spanish. The printed version runs 27 pages and must be single-sided. The county doesn't process an application until you appear in person with ID and sign it.

That happens at the Prothonotary's Family Court Office in the Bucks County Justice Center, 100 North Main Street, Doylestown. The office is open Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:15 p.m., and completed petitions are accepted until 2:30 p.m. Arrive early if you want a judge to hear the petition that day. The Family Prothonotary's number is 215-348-6822.

After hours, on weekends and on holidays, the county directs people to a Magisterial District Judge's office. Local police can tell you which judge is on call.

Two sources of help are named on the county page. The A Woman's Place legal advocacy program, at 215-348-0445, helps with forms and goes to court with you. Legal Aid of Southeastern Pennsylvania has a Doylestown office at 70 W. Oakland Avenue, Lower Level, Suite 106, phone 215-340-1818, and the District Attorney's list gives a Bristol number, 215-781-1111. The county says free attorneys for PFA hearings are available through Legal Aid and the Bucks County Bar Association.

When home isn't an option tonight

Emergency shelter for domestic violence goes through the A Woman's Place hotline. For other housing emergencies, the Bucks County Housing Link is the county's central point for assessment and referral. Its helpline, 1-888-50-BUCKS, is run by Family Service Association, and voicemail is available at all hours. The county's hotline page lists a second Housing Link number, 1-800-810-4434.

PA 211 covers everything else, from food to utilities. Dial 211 or text your ZIP code to 898-211. The service is free, confidential and staffed every day of the year.

Older women and women with disabilities

Abuse later in life often comes from a caregiver or an adult child, and it has its own reporting line. The Bucks County Area Agency on Aging lists a 24-hour protective services hotline at 1-800-243-3767. The District Attorney's office says the agency serves people 60 and older. Its office is at 55 E. Court Street, 3rd Floor, Doylestown, open weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., at 267-880-5700.

The county's Adult Protective Services page gives a separate number, 1-800-490-8505, for reporting abuse of an older adult or an adult with an intellectual disability or autism. The domestic violence and victim hotlines above serve women of every ability. If you need an interpreter or another accommodation, say so at the start of the call.

LGBTQ+ young women

Abuse happens in relationships of every kind, and the hotlines above are open to anyone. For younger people who want an affirming room first, Planned Parenthood Keystone runs The Rainbow Room for LGBTQ+ youth ages 14 to 21. It meets Wednesdays from 6 to 8 p.m. at 186 E. Court Street in Doylestown and Tuesdays from 6 to 8 p.m. at 334 S. Bellevue Avenue in Langhorne. Twelve- and 13-year-olds are welcome in Doylestown on the fourth Wednesday of the month. The program's number is 267-282-4117.

The national lines and the 3 a.m. hours

The county's hotline page lists the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. It's a good option if you're traveling or helping a friend in another state.

Living with abuse wears on mental health. The 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline takes calls and texts at 988 and offers online chat. Locally, the county lists Lenape Valley Foundation's mobile crisis line at 1-877-435-7709, with crisis centers in Central Bucks at 215-345-2273 and Lower Bucks at 215-785-9765.

Quick answers

Do I need a police report to call A Woman's Place or NOVA?

No. Neither listing we checked requires one. Both hotlines are described as free and confidential, and you can call to ask questions without deciding anything.

Can I file for a PFA on a weekend?

Yes. The Family Court office in Doylestown keeps weekday hours, but the county says a Magisterial District Judge handles emergency petitions after hours, on weekends and on holidays. Call the police non-emergency line to learn which judge is on call.

What should I bring to the Justice Center?

Bring photo ID, since the county won't process a petition without it. A petition completed ahead of time, online or on paper, saves time at the counter. Plan to arrive well before the 2:30 p.m. cutoff.

How can I help a friend I'm worried about?

Listen, believe her and let her set the pace. You can call either local hotline yourself to ask an advocate how to support her, and you can offer your phone or computer if hers isn't private.
