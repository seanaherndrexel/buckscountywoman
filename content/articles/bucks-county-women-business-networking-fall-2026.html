Three chambers of commerce cover Bucks County, and every one of them runs a committee built for women in business. Add a standalone women's alliance that meets over lunch every month, a Philadelphia chapter of the country's largest women business owners' association, and an LGBTQ+ chamber with its own women's programming, and the county's calendar for October through December is fuller than most people realize.

We checked every group below against its own website on October 1, 2026. Where an organizer has posted specific fall dates, we list them. Where it hasn't, we give the standing schedule so you can plan around it. Prices weren't posted for most of these events, so confirm costs with the host before you register.

The Chamber Committees

Lower Bucks County Chamber of Commerce: Women in Business Committee

The Lower Bucks Chamber's Women in Business Committee meets on the first Wednesday of every month at 9:00 a.m. That puts the fall meetings on October 7, November 4 and December 2 if the pattern holds. Co-chairs Shikha Patel and Lorena Escalera lead the group, and the chamber describes its purpose as providing awareness, education, resources and networking "from a woman's perspective."

The same chamber also runs a b.o.s.s. (Business Owners: Support & Strategies) Roundtable on the first Tuesday of each month at 8:30 a.m. It isn't women-only, but solo founders who want a small peer group to work through real problems should look at it alongside the women's committee.

Upper Bucks Chamber of Commerce: LEAP

Up in Quakertown country, the Upper Bucks Chamber's LEAP committee meets on the third Thursday of each month from 9:30 to 10:30 a.m. The chamber says the group exists to foster the growth of women entrepreneurs and professionals in its community, with an emphasis on collaboration and inclusivity. On the standing schedule, fall meetings fall on October 15, November 19 and December 17.

Central Bucks Chamber of Commerce: Women in Business

Doylestown's chamber calls its Women in Business Committee its largest. Monthly meetings typically include a 20-minute speaker panel, raffles and refreshments, and members can join subcommittees covering events, a leadership forum, marketing, mentorship and the monthly meetings themselves.

Here's the catch for this season. The committee has posted that it's taking a break this fall to reassess member needs and align its calendar with the chamber's fiscal year, and it plans to reconvene in January 2027. You need to be a Central Bucks Chamber member to join, though prospective members can attend a meeting first. If you're in the Doylestown area and want to be in the room when it restarts, now is a sensible time to get your chamber membership sorted. The contact listed is Sue LaRosa at the chamber office.

A Monthly Lunch in New Hope, and a Holiday Party in Newtown

The Bucks County Business Women's Alliance is the most event-dense option on this list for the fall. The nonprofit meets on the third Friday of the month for an 11:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. lunch with networking, a keynote speaker, Q&A and member spotlights, and it adds happy hours through the year. Membership is for women business owners and professionals in Bucks County and the surrounding area.

Its posted fall schedule:

  • October 16, 2026, 11:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.: "AI Workflows for Business" at Jericho National Golf Club, 250 Brownsburg Road East, New Hope.
  • November 20, 2026, 11:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.: "Podcasting for Business Growth: How to Host and Guest Like a Pro" at The Fire One in Warrington, 2099 Maple Avenue, Warrington.
  • December 9, 2026, 5:30 to 7:30 p.m.: the alliance's holiday networking soiree at The Blue Point Grill, 3 West Road, Newtown.

Ticket prices weren't listed on the events page, so RSVP through the individual event links to see costs.

Longtime residents may remember the Women's Business Forum of Bucks County. It closed after 15 years with a farewell celebration at Stonebrook Farm in Perkasie, so you won't find new meetings under that name.

Regional Groups Worth the Drive

NAWBO Philadelphia

The National Association of Women Business Owners' Philadelphia chapter runs a busy fall slate, mostly in Montgomery and Delaware counties and in Philadelphia. Its "Power Friday and Beyond" series covers practical owner topics, and the chapter has two marquee October events on its calendar:

  • October 15, 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.: "Thrive HER," a women's health experience at Temple University's Ambler campus, 580 Meetinghouse Road, Ambler.
  • October 16, 1:00 to 4:00 p.m.: "She Pitches Philly," the chapter's women's pitch competition at Villanova University.

Further Power Friday sessions are posted for October 14 in Wayne ("Integrating Work and Life in Ways that Make Sense for YOU"), October 23 ("Build a Business that Runs Without YOU"), November 6 (turning holiday appreciation into business), and December 18 ("AI: Friend or Foe?"). Ambler is a short drive from Doylestown and Warminster, which makes Thrive HER the most accessible of the big events for Central Bucks owners.

Independence Business Alliance

The Independence Business Alliance is Greater Philadelphia's LGBTQ+ chamber of commerce and an official affiliate of the National LGBT Chamber of Commerce. It serves LGBTQ+ business owners, professionals and allies across the tri-state area, and its networking events cover LGBTBE certification for LGBTQ+-owned firms.

On Wednesday, October 28, 2026, the alliance hosts its Women in Business Breakfast, "The Solopreneur Mindset: Expanding Courageous Ecosystems," at Quorum at the University City Science Center in Philadelphia. The organizers pitch it as candid conversation and networking for women across industries and career stages, whether they're building companies or leading inside one.

Free Advisers and Classroom Options

Networking gets you introductions. The groups below get you a business plan, a cash flow model or a second set of eyes on a loan application, and most of it costs nothing.

Lehigh University Small Business Development Center. Lehigh's SBDC lists Bucks County in its service area and offers no-cost, confidential consulting to small business owners and people preparing to start one.

SCORE. SCORE's Greater Philadelphia network covers the Delaware Valley, including Bucks, and its volunteer mentoring is free for the life of your business.

Women's Business Enterprise Center East. WBEC East runs an SBA-funded Women's Business Center serving the Greater Philadelphia area. Programs include startup courses such as Entrepreneur Readiness and SBA Launchpad, one-on-one counseling, self-paced online classes and help preparing for certification. The center notes that many startup programs are funded through its cooperative agreement with the SBA's Office of Women's Business Ownership.

Bucks County Community College. Bucks offers a 19-credit Entrepreneurship Certificate covering business planning, finance, business law and social media marketing, with classes at the Newtown, Bristol and Perkasie campuses and online. For side-hustlers who want structure without a full degree, it's a practical option.

Getting on the Approved Vendor List

Certification won't land a contract by itself, but it puts a woman-owned business in front of buyers who are required or motivated to look for one. There are three main paths for Bucks County firms.

WBENC certification

The Women's Business Enterprise National Council is the certification many large corporations look for in their supplier programs. A business must be at least 51 percent owned, managed and controlled by one or more women who are U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents, and a woman must hold the top title. Expect a document review and a site visit. Fees run on a revenue scale, starting at $350 for businesses under $1 million and rising to $1,250 for companies above $50 million, with a 3 percent processing fee on credit card applications since July 1, 2026. WBEC East handles certification for Pennsylvania.

SBA Women-Owned Small Business program

The federal government aims to award 5 percent of prime contracting dollars to women-owned small businesses. The SBA's WOSB program requires 51 percent ownership and control by women who are U.S. citizens, with women running day-to-day operations and long-term decisions. The economically disadvantaged tier (EDWOSB) adds limits: personal net worth under $850,000, average adjusted gross income of $400,000 or less over three years, and personal assets of $6.5 million or less. You apply through the SBA's online certification portal, and the agency aims to decide within 90 days of a complete application.

Pennsylvania's Small Diverse Business program

For state contracts, Pennsylvania's Department of General Services verifies Small Diverse Businesses. A firm qualifies when it's at least 51 percent owned by women, minorities, people with disabilities or LGBTQIA+ owners, holds a valid third-party certificate and meets the state's small business size standards. Plain small business self-certification is approved immediately, so many owners start there and add the diverse designation once a third-party certificate comes through.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to own a business to join these groups?

No. Most of the chamber committees and the Business Women's Alliance welcome professionals and employees as well as owners. You will generally need to be a member of the chamber itself to join its committee, though several let you visit once before you commit.

Which group is best if I only have a side hustle?

Start with the free resources: an SBDC consultation or a SCORE mentor will help you decide whether the side hustle is ready to grow. For people, a monthly lunch like the Business Women's Alliance or a chamber committee near home gives you a steady room of familiar faces, which matters more than any single big event.

Is WBENC certification worth $350 for a small company?

It depends on who you sell to. If your buyers are large corporations or institutions with supplier diversity goals, it can get you onto lists you'd never reach otherwise. If you sell mostly to local consumers, the money is probably better spent on marketing for now.

Are there LGBTQ+-specific options for women in business here?

Yes. The Independence Business Alliance serves the whole Greater Philadelphia region, runs its own women's programming, and its events cover LGBTBE certification. Pennsylvania's Small Diverse Business program also includes LGBTQIA+-owned firms that hold a valid third-party certificate.
