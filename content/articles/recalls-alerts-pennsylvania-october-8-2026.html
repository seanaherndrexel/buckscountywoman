Ten notices went up on the FDA's main recall page between October 2 and October 7. Half of them cover products sold in Pennsylvania stores or shipped to buyers nationwide, and one names two chains Bucks County shoppers use: Walmart and Trader Joe's.

We opened each notice on the agency's own site and summarized only what it says. Lot codes decide whether your package is included, so use the link under each item before you throw anything out. The running list lives on the Food Safety Watch page.

Start with the freezer

Great Value and Trader Joe's organic frozen berries

On October 2, Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A. expanded an earlier recall of frozen berries because of potential contamination with E. coli O145:H28. The company calls the step precautionary and says it is based on traceback information tied to frozen blueberries first recalled on July 3. Three products were added:

  • Great Value Organic Triple Berry Blend, 10 oz, UPC 7874211226, lot codes 6 031 01 and 6 041 01, best if used by January 31, 2028 and February 10, 2028.
  • Great Value Organic Blueberries, 10 oz, UPC 7874211213, lot codes 6 019 01, 6 040 01 and 6 163 01, best if used by January 19, February 9 and June 12, 2028.
  • Trader Joe's Organic Mixed Berry Blend, 12 oz, UPC 00977654, lot code 6 051 01, best before February 20, 2028.

The Great Value bags went to select Walmart stores in 30 states, Pennsylvania among them. The Trader Joe's bags went to select stores in 26 states, also including Pennsylvania. The notice doesn't report confirmed illnesses from these lots. It tells shoppers not to eat the berries and to discard them or return them to the store for a full refund. Read the FDA notice.

The cheese drawer and the seed jar

Graziers raw milk cheese

Sierra Nevada Cheese Company announced a recall of all Graziers Raw Milk Cheese in distribution on September 28, and the FDA posted it the next day. The reason given is potential contamination with Shiga toxin-producing E. coli O26:H11. The recall covers Monterey Jack, Jalapeño Jack, Medium Cheddar and Sharp Cheddar in 8 oz squares, a 16 oz Medium Cheddar brick, and larger food service loaves and blocks. No lot codes are listed because, in the company's words, the investigation has "not been narrowed to a specific lot, date code, or production date."

The cheese was distributed nationwide through distributors, retailers and direct customers. The notice doesn't name stores. It says the FDA and CDC are investigating a multistate outbreak with 13 illnesses identified. The FDA's outbreak advisory, updated September 25, counts eight hospitalizations and lists cases in nine states. Pennsylvania isn't one of them. The FDA's advice: throw the cheese away or return it, including any piece that has lost its original wrapper, and clean the surfaces and containers it touched. Recall notice and FDA outbreak advisory.

Broccoli seeds for sprouting

Two online seed sellers recalled Calabrese broccoli seeds for possible Salmonella contamination. If you grow sprouts or microgreens on the windowsill, check your packets.

  • Eden Brothers Broccoli Seeds, Calabrese (packet, 1 ounce and 1 pound), lot numbers 1022067 and 1022068, sold on EdenBrothers.com and Amazon from July 8 through September 28 and shipped nationwide. Posted October 7. No illnesses reported. The company says to seal the seeds in a bag, put them in the trash and not the compost, and wash anything they touched. FDA notice
  • HOME GROWN Green Sprouting Calabrese Broccoli Seeds, 8 oz, UPC 850056627443, lot AUG172026, sold on Amazon nationwide from August 20 to September 18. Posted October 2. The company reports no illnesses from its product and directs buyers to Amazon for a refund. FDA notice

Ingredients that weren't on the label

No new lead or heavy metal recall appeared on the FDA's page this week. The September 24 recall of Lior ground cinnamon and the FDA's standing cinnamon and cookware alerts remain on our Food Safety Watch page. Two chemical findings did come in, both from FDA testing of supplements.

Babies' Magic Tea Baby Sleep Gripe Water

Pacific Health Sciences recalled one lot of this infant supplement, a 4 fl oz amber bottle in a blue box, after FDA testing found undeclared ethanol (about 8.3%), methanol (about 0.35%) and isopropanol (about 0.48%). The lot is 25122bw, best before 12/2028, printed on the bottom of the box and the side of the bottle. It was sold online, including at secretsoftea.com and on Amazon. The company says it has received no reports of adverse events for the lot. Parents are told to stop using it, discard or return it, and seek medical attention if a child shows symptoms after use. FDA notice

RAPTURE Preworkout powder

Leader Formulas, LLC. recalled lot 260327-4 of this 450 g workout supplement in Rainbow Twist, Electric Lemonade and Berry Mojito flavors because it contains 1,3-dimethylamylamine, known as DMAA. The notice says DMAA can raise blood pressure and may cause cardiovascular problems. The powder was distributed nationwide through retail stores and the company's website from January 29, 2025 to May 25, 2026. No adverse events have been reported to the company. FDA notice

From the toy bin and the nursery

The Consumer Product Safety Commission's most recent batch is dated October 1. Two entries concern young children and were sold online to buyers anywhere.

  • GBYMIUY Magnetic Stick Figure Toy Sets. About 10,750 packs of 30 figures sold on Amazon from April through July 2026 for about $20. The CPSC says the toys "can liberate loose high-powered magnets posing an ingestion hazard to children." No injuries reported. The remedy is a full refund after you email a photo of the discarded toys. CPSC notice
  • Mbabyjon padded crib bumpers. This is a warning, not a recall. The CPSC says the bumpers, sold at Mbabyjon.myshopify.com for $15 to $28, "can obstruct an infant's breathing," and that the seller has not responded to its request for a recall. No incidents reported. The agency urges parents to stop using them and dispose of them. CPSC warning

Headlines you can cross off

Three other recalls posted this week were not distributed in Pennsylvania, according to the notices. Nature's Own Hawaiian Bread, recalled for undeclared milk, went to Arkansas, Louisiana, New Mexico, Oklahoma and Texas. Stewart's Shops half-gallon ice cream, recalled for possible metal, was sold in New York, Vermont and New Hampshire. Saint Francis frozen pizzas, recalled for undeclared soy, were sold in Ohio and Kentucky and online. If you carried any of these home from a trip, the notices on the FDA recall page have the codes.

The Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture's latest news releases, dated September 24 through October 7, contain no consumer food warnings.

Ten minutes with your own shelves

  1. Match the code, not the brand. Find the UPC under the barcode and the lot code or best-by date stamped on the package. A product is covered only when those match the notice.
  2. Don't taste it to check. Bacteria and chemical contaminants don't change how food looks or smells.
  3. Take a photo first. Photograph the front of the package and the code before you discard it. Some firms ask for a photo as proof.
  4. Follow the refund route in the notice. Store brands usually go back to the store's service desk. The berry notice says to return bags to Walmart or Trader Joe's for a full refund. Online purchases typically go through the seller or the company's phone line or form.
  5. Clean up. Wash your hands and wipe down shelves, drawers and containers the product touched.
  6. Report problems. Product injuries go to SaferProducts.gov. Reactions to supplements go to FDA MedWatch at 1-800-332-1088.

Quick answers

Do I need a receipt to get my money back?

The notices we read this week don't mention receipts. They say to return the product to the place of purchase or contact the company. Bring the package if you still have it, and call the store first if you don't.

I already ate some of the recalled berries. What now?

The notice lists severe stomach cramps, bloody diarrhea and vomiting as symptoms of this type of E. coli infection. If you have symptoms or concerns, call your healthcare provider. This article is information, not medical advice.

Why are garden seeds on a food recall list?

Broccoli seeds are often sprouted and eaten raw. One of the two companies says its supplier's seeds were used by a sprout grower whose products were part of a multistate illness outbreak the FDA investigated, and that the FDA later detected Salmonella in a seed sample.

How do I hear about recalls sooner?

The FDA and the CPSC both offer free email alerts from their recall pages, and the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service does the same for meat, poultry and egg products. We also update Food Safety Watch every week.
