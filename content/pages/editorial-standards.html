The rules we work by

You're trusting us with decisions about your health, your safety and your money. These are the standards we hold ourselves to, written plainly so you can check our work against them.

Where our facts come from

  • We go to the source. Phone numbers, addresses, hours and services are taken from the organization's own website or confirmed with the organization directly.
  • For health reporting, we rely on primary sources such as government health agencies, peer-reviewed research, professional medical societies and the providers themselves.
  • We link to our sources so you can read them yourself.
  • We say what we don't know. If we couldn't confirm something, we leave it out or tell you it's unconfirmed.
  • We date our reporting. Local services change, and an article tells you when its details were last checked.
  • We don't invent quotes, people, statistics or studies.

How we use AI

Our content is researched and drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by an editor before it's published. We'd rather tell you that directly than have you wonder.

In practice, AI tools help us gather public information, check it against sources and write first drafts. An editor then reviews the work, and a person is responsible for everything that runs under our name. Facts that people rely on, especially phone numbers, addresses and health information, are checked against the original source. If an AI tool gets something wrong and we publish it, that's our error, and we correct it the same way we'd correct any other.

We don't publish AI-generated images of real people or real places as if they were photographs.

Corrections

When we get something wrong, we fix it fast and we say so. A corrected article carries a note explaining what changed and when. We don't quietly rewrite errors of fact.

To request a correction, use the contact page or email sean@mainstreetmakes.com. Corrections to crisis lines and other safety information are handled first.

Health content

Our health coverage is information, not medical advice. We report on where care is available in Bucks County and what current guidance says, and we work hard to get it right. We aren't your doctor, and nothing we publish replaces a conversation with a qualified clinician who knows your history. Please don't delay care or change a treatment because of something you read here. In an emergency, call 911.

We don't publish claims of cures, and we don't promote treatments that lack credible evidence.

Independence from advertisers

Advertisers and sponsors don't decide what we cover or how we cover it. Buying an ad doesn't earn a favorable article, and declining to advertise never counts against a business. Advertisers don't see articles before they're published.

The same rule applies to the organizations that receive money through our giving pledge. We cover them as we'd cover anyone else.

Sponsored content is labeled

Anything that was paid for is marked "Sponsored" or "Advertisement" where you can see it, before you start reading. That includes sponsored articles, sponsored directory listings and sponsored sections of the newsletter. If it isn't labeled, nobody paid for it.

What we don't publish

We don't publish religious content or partisan politics, and we don't accept advertising for either. We cover Bucks County only.

Fairness and privacy

  • We write for every woman in the county and describe people the way they describe themselves.
  • We don't publish the names of people who've been harmed without their clear permission.
  • We protect people who send us tips in confidence.
  • If we write critically about a person or business, we ask them for their side first.

Other people's work

We credit photographers and writers, and we use photos only with permission or a proper license. In Local Finds, Instagram posts appear only through Instagram's official embed, with the creator credited and tagged. If your post is featured and you want it removed, contact us and we'll take it down.
