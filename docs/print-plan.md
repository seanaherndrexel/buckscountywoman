# Bucks County Woman: Print Plan

Prepared October 1, 2026 for Sean Ahern, Publisher. Plain-language plan to get the first printed issue (Spring 2027) on the street.

---

## 0. Read this first: the name

Two findings need a decision before any money goes out.

1. **Bucks County Women's Journal already exists and is active.** It is a free print publication for Bucks County women, published six times a year, claims 40,000 readers, and places copies in the exact spots we would use (salons, gyms, coffee shops, medical offices, libraries, banks, gourmet shops). Publisher: Robin Coppinger, 215-872-1814. Source: [buckscountywomensjournal.com/advertising-information](https://buckscountywomensjournal.com/advertising-information/). Our name is one word away from theirs. Advertisers and readers will confuse the two, and they could claim we are trading on their name.
2. **"BUCKS COUNTY WOMAN" was filed as a magazine trademark in 2008** by Phoenix Global Media (serial 77614885). It was **abandoned in 2009**, so it does not block us today, but it shows someone has tried this exact title before. Source: [Justia Trademarks](https://trademarks.justia.com/776/14/bucks-county-woman-77614885.html).

**Recommendation:** Pick up the phone or have a trademark attorney do a one-hour clearance on "Bucks County Woman" against "Bucks County Women's Journal" before printing a logo. If the risk looks real, a distinct title (for example "BCW" as a brand mark with a different full name) costs nothing now and a lot later. Everything below works with any name.

## 1. Recommended launch format

| Item | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Quarterly** (Spring, Summer, Fall, Holiday) | Matches Bucks County Magazine, the strongest local title (17,000 copies, seasonal quarterly). Four issues a year gives ad sales time to work and keeps cash needs low in year one. |
| Trim size | **8.375 x 10.875 in.** | The standard US magazine size. Every printer below runs it on standard sheets, so no waste penalty. Fits standard magazine racks. |
| Page count | **36 pages (32 inside + 4-page cover)** | Big enough to feel like a real magazine, small enough to fill with launch ad sales. Page counts must go up in multiples of 4 (or 8/16 on larger presses), so 40 or 48 is the next step once ads grow. |
| Paper | **Cover: 80# or 100# gloss cover. Inside: 70# gloss text.** | Gloss makes photos and ads look premium, which is what women's lifestyle advertisers (salons, med spas, boutiques, realtors) pay for. |
| Binding | **Saddle-stitched** (stapled on the spine) | Cheapest and standard below about 64 pages. |
| Color | Full color (4/4) throughout | Expected in this category. |
| Print run | **Start at 5,000. Price 10,000 as an option.** | 5,000 is enough to fill 150 to 200 pickup spots plus events without leftovers. Price per copy drops sharply at 10,000, so we go there once ad revenue covers it or if we add mailing. |

**Mailing note (important for cost):** USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) caps each piece at **3.3 ounces**. The premium spec above weighs roughly 4.5 oz, so it does **not** qualify. A mail-friendly version is 32 pages, self-cover (cover on the same paper as the inside), 60# gloss text, which comes in near 3 oz. My estimate, the printer will confirm. So: premium spec for pickup racks, lighter spec if we mail.

## 2. What it costs

### Printing (estimate, pending the quotes we are requesting)

| Run | Est. cost per copy | Est. total per issue |
|---|---|---|
| 5,000 copies, 36 pp, gloss | $1.00 to $1.60 | **$5,000 to $8,000** |
| 10,000 copies, 36 pp, gloss | $0.70 to $1.10 | **$7,000 to $11,000** |

These ranges are my estimate built from published pricing: Greenerprinter shows a 40-page saddle-stitched magazine at $2.32 per copy for 2,000 copies and says most mid-size runs fall between $3 and $6 per copy on online pricing ([source](https://www.greenerprinter.com/blog/magazine-printing-costs-how-much-does-it-cost-to-print-a-magazine/)); C&D Printing shows per-copy cost dropping roughly in half from 1,000 to 5,000 copies on offset presses ([source](https://cndprinting.com/magazine-printing-cost/)). Regional offset printers usually beat online printers at 5,000+. The five quote requests will replace these estimates with real numbers within about two weeks.

What doubling the run does: the second 5,000 copies typically costs only 40 to 60 percent of the first 5,000, because setup (plates, makeready) is paid once.

### Getting it to readers

| Method | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Free pickup racks** | Racks $25 to $60 each (countertop acrylic) if the location has none. Delivery is our own time or a part-time driver, roughly $200 to $400 per issue. | Cheapest. Readers who pick it up chose to, so advertisers value them. |
| **EDDM Retail (mail to every home on chosen routes)** | **$0.26 per piece postage** (2026 rate), plus printer bundling/facing slips, about $0.03 to $0.06 per piece. No permit needed. Max 5,000 pieces per ZIP per day. | 5,000 homes mailed = about **$1,450 to $1,600 per issue** on top of printing. Requires the lighter 3.3 oz spec. Sources: [Mail Processing Associates](https://www.mailpro.org/post/every-door-direct-mail-eddm/), [Cornerstone](https://crst.net/services/eddm/how-much-does-eddm-cost). |
| **EDDM via printer's permit (BMEU)** | Lower postage per piece, $370/yr permit, or use the printer's permit. | Worth it once mailing 10,000+ per issue. Garrison, Vizi and Magagna all run mail shops. |

**Year-one budget per issue at 5,000 copies, pickup only:** about $6,000 to $9,000 for print and distribution, before design and writing. With 5,000 mailed on top, add about $1,500.

## 4. Distribution plan

**Goal for issue 1:** 150 to 200 pickup locations across central and upper Bucks, about 25 to 35 copies each, plus 500 to 1,000 copies held for events and advertiser counters.

Where to place, and who to ask:

| Location type | Who to ask | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Public libraries | Branch manager at each Bucks County Free Library branch (Doylestown HQ first) | Libraries often have a community literature table; ask about their policy. |
| Coffee shops and cafes | Owner or manager | Counter racks near the register. Offer a free listing in the directory in exchange. |
| Salons, spas, nail and lash studios | Owner | The single best spot for this audience: long wait times. Many will also advertise. |
| Gyms, yoga, Pilates and barre studios | Studio owner or front desk lead | Lobby racks. |
| Women-owned boutiques and gift shops | Owner | Natural advertiser pool; ask them to stock and advertise. |
| Medical, dental, OB/GYN, dermatology and med spa waiting rooms | Office manager | Long dwell time. Large health systems need a marketing-department approval; independent practices decide on the spot. |
| Realtor offices, banks, chamber offices | Branch or office manager | The Central Bucks Chamber can share copies with members. |
| Grocery and gourmet stores | Store manager; corporate approval for chains | Independents are easier than chains. |
| Events | Event organizers (farmers markets, Doylestown Arts Festival, women's business networking groups, charity runs) | Hand out copies at a branded table. |

**How it works each quarter:** keep a simple spreadsheet of every location with contact name and copy count. Drop issues within 3 days of delivery from the printer. Check pickup rates at the 6-week mark and move copies from slow spots to fast ones. Every advertiser gets a stack for their counter.

**EDDM (optional, from issue 2):** mail to the wealthiest carrier routes in Doylestown, New Hope, Newtown, Yardley and Solebury using the USPS EDDM mapping tool. Requires the lighter mail spec.

## 7. Legal and admin checklist

| Item | Cost | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Name clearance / trademark** | Search free on [USPTO.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/); attorney clearance about $300 to $750; federal filing $350 per class | Search "Bucks County Woman" and similar. ## 8. Honest risk note

Print magazines are a sales business wearing a publishing costume: every issue costs $6,000 to $9,000 to print and distribute before a single page of design or writing, and that bill is due whether or not the ads sold, so the first three issues will live or die on how many advertisers Sean signs before the January 22 deadline. The bigger strategic risk is the name and the competitor: Bucks County Women's Journal is already established with the same audience, the same distribution spots and a nearly identical name, which means some advertisers will ask why they should pay twice and some readers will assume we are them. The safest path is to clear the name first, commit to only 5,000 copies until two issues have proven the ad base, require prepayment from new advertisers, and set a go/no-go rule: if signed ads do not cover the print bill by January 22, push the launch to Summer 2027 rather than print at a loss.
